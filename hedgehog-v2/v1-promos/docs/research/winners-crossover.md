## 2. Transferable patterns (with evidence)

1. **Accessibility for one named disability wins grand prizes year after year.** Grand-prize winners include Shepherd (blind, TreeHacks 2026) and Baymax (paralysis/Parkinson's, TreeHacks 2024). BlinkAI, ROSS (blind users and art) and PathSense (indoor navigation) also placed. Judges reward "innovation with empathy" (Stanford Daily, 2024). The best of these demos show a body using the product, not a dashboard. **Strongest evidence for Earshot.**
2. **Something physical or audible on stage beats anything shown on a screen.** Examples: a cane, a robot arm, an EEG headset (Duet), a dissolution tester (Peel), a broom (Maestro), a Nerf gun, motorized chess pieces, and two phones beeping at each other (GibberLink). Even a cheap prop (a phone, a speaker, a QR card) turns a demo from "watch my screen" into an event.
3. **Voice agents win when they act in the real world, not just chat.** Procuro phoned suppliers, Dealwise phoned businesses for quotes, Roadmate texts a friend, and GibberLink was a real call. ElevenLabs judges promoted the agents that *did something consequential* over the assistants.
4. **One hard number frames the problem.** Peel: the WHO says 1 in 10 medicines are falsified. CrossBeam: more than 90% of permit applications are rejected on first submission. TARA: weeks of work cut to hours. Winners open with a stat that sets the stakes before the demo starts.
5. **"It just works" beats "it does everything."** Voxy's winning judge quote was "The platform just works." The Tango finalist pitched only reliability. Hawkwatch is a simple loop: alert, then dismiss or escalate. Tight, reliable flows in front of judges win.
6. **The reverse direction is a novelty hook.** Duet went brain to music when research usually goes music to brain. GibberLink had AIs talk to each other instead of to humans. Angel's Protection found people by their clothes instead of their faces. One inverted assumption gives judges a sentence to repeat to each other.
7. **One build can stack several prizes.** Angel's Protection won three first places at HackTX 2024. Peel won the grand prize plus a sponsor track. At Texas events, a single build aimed at 2-3 sponsor criteria is standard. This matches UBC's track plus bounty structure.
8. **Texas judging names design explicitly.** HackTX 2025's Best Overall criteria are "design, complexity, and impact," and there is a separate Best Design prize. HackWesTX gives a Best UI/UX prize. Polish counts locally.

## 3. Borrowable mechanics and demo tricks

| # | Mechanic / trick | Seen in | Use it for |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Blindfold the judge.** A judge (or a teammate in a sleep mask) completes a purchase by voice alone. The USDC transfer lands on Arc and the confirmation is spoken back. | Shepherd, Baymax, ROSS, BlinkAI (the judge tries it) | **A Earshot** (primary). Also D Spendthrift: a voice reads out "new payee, holding 20 s" |
| 2 | **Two machines talking audibly.** Stage two agents (buyer agent and seller agent, or user agent and escrow) whose exchange the room can *hear*, for example via ElevenLabs voices, before settlement. | GibberLink, Procuro, Dealwise | **A**, **B** (the agent "calls" a tipster), **F** Hunch (the robot auctioneer speaks its bids through ElevenLabs) |
| 3 | **The audience scans a QR code and participates.** Hack the North runs People's Choice this way; we would bring it into the demo itself. Judges' phones submit tips or price guesses, and the leaderboard and payouts update live. | HTN People's Choice; GibberLink (2 phones) | **B** Edge402 (judges tip from phones, built in), **F** Hunch (room vs robot), **G** Proviso (the room bids on a table) |
| 4 | **Open with one hard statistic.** One number on the first slide before the demo. | Peel (WHO 1 in 10), CrossBeam (90%+ rejected) | **A** (blind shoppers' checkout failure rate; source needed), **C** Shed (ERCOT emergency MW / Winter Storm Uri), **E** Unknot (invoice financing gap) |
| 5 | **A human-in-the-loop alert with dismiss or escalate.** Show the alert, a person decides, and the system acts. | Hawkwatch, Roadmate (texts a friend) | **D** Spendthrift (the 20 s hold pings a trusted contact who can cancel), **C** (the grid-emergency alert bumps compute jobs with a visible override) |
| 6 | **Reverse the obvious direction.** Write a one-sentence inversion for the pitch. | Duet, GibberLink, Angel's Protection | **B** ("the AI pays *you* only if you beat it"), **C** ("an airline bump list, but for GPUs"), **F** ("the auctioneer is a robot and the room is the bidder") |
| 7 | **A physical prop for an abstract system.** A cheap object makes the invisible visible. | Maestro (broom), Peel ($30 box), S-KBD67 (Nerf) | **C** (a desk lamp or fan as "the data center" that powers down when the grid alert fires) [?], **G** (a printed table tent with a QR rent meter) |
| 8 | **Stack 2-3 sponsor criteria in one flow.** Each sponsor gets a visible moment in the 5-minute demo. | Angel's Protection (3 firsts), Peel | **A** (Arc + ElevenLabs + Circle), **B** (x402 + Gemini), **F/G** (Gemini prices + chain) |
| 9 | **An origin story with one named person.** | Elisa (daughter), CrossBeam (lawyer) | **A** (a specific blind shopper persona or interview, if real; do not invent one), **E** (a named small supplier waiting 60 days to be paid) |
| 10 | **Close with "it just works": rerun the flow cold.** Run the whole flow a second time quickly at the end to prove it is reliable. | Voxy, Tango | All; especially **A** and **D**, where trust is the product |

## 4. Bottom line for our shortlist
- **Earshot (A)** has the strongest crossover evidence. Accessibility, voice and a real action that moves money each appear among grand-prize winners at non-crypto events (Shepherd, Baymax, PathSense, ROSS). Use the blindfold demo plus an audible agent exchange.
- **Edge402 (B), Hunch (F) and Proviso (G)** borrow most from audience participation (the HTN QR mechanism). I found no direct non-crypto winners in prediction markets or sports betting, so the evidence for these is weaker.
- **Shed (C)** needs a physical prop and a hard grid statistic to beat the "it's just a dashboard" problem.
