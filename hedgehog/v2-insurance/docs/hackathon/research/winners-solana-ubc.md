# What wins: Solana/Colosseum, UBC (ex-MBC), and student blockchain hackathons

Researched 2026-11-18. Web research only. Items marked **(unverified)** came from secondary or AI-generated sources, or the page could not be read in full.

## 0. UBC lineage (important context)

- The University Blockchain Conference is the renamed **Midwest Blockchain Conference (MBC)**. MBC 2024 and MBC 2025 were both held at the University of Michigan. UBC 2026 is at UT Austin with Texas Blockchain, Nov 20-21. Sources: https://luma.com/n4ad0k9m, https://i2s-research.ku.edu/news/article/KUBI-ETHDenver-2025
- **MBC 2024** (U. Michigan): more than 1,000 attendees from 30+ universities. Colosseum spoke there, and the "Intro to Solana" session was packed while the Sei and Sui sessions drew far fewer people. No hackathon winners were published in that recap. https://blog.colosseum.com/radar-hackathon-winners-midwest-blockchain-conference-mfv/
- **MBC Hackathon 2025** (Nov 30 - Dec 5, 2025, Ann Arbor): $40k pool. Tracks were **Solana** (1st $7.5k, places 1-5) and **Base**, plus optional **Polymarket** prediction-markets ($3.5k/$1.5k) and **Circle** bounties. The top 10 pitched live at the closing ceremony. Stated emphasis: "technical depth, originality, strong UX, and real-world impact." https://mbc.devpost.com/, https://allhackathons.com/hackathon/midwest-blockchain-conference-hackathon-2025
- **The MBC 2025 winners could not be retrieved.** Devpost returns 403 to automated fetches, and no recap, X post or LinkedIn post naming winners turned up. **Action for the team:** open https://mbc.devpost.com/project-gallery in a browser. It is the single most relevant data point, because the same organizers run UBC and likely draw on a similar judge pool.
- MBC also ran a virtual Avalanche hackathon in Jan 2025: 1st Imit8, 2nd Memeverse **(unverified, names only)**.
- The UBC 2026 Devpost listing gives a ~$22k pool, is student-only and lasts one week (hackathonradar listing).

## (b) Patterns: what wins, what loses

1. **Payments for AI agents is the dominant winning theme from 2025 on.** Latinum (Breakout AI 1st), MCPay (Cypherpunk Stablecoin 1st), Corbits, Mercantill, all five x402 main-track winners, and the Arc/Cannes agentic winners all fit it. Arc reports that 98% of its Cannes submissions were agentic, and that Circle Nanopayments was core infrastructure for about 67% of winners. *Implication:* the theme is a strong fit, but it is crowded, so differentiation has to come from the use case, not the plumbing.
2. **A concrete, everyday or physical use case beats an abstract "agent framework."** Examples: PlaiPin (an ESP32 chip with a wallet; it won at both x402 and Cypherpunk), Onda (pays the artist you're hearing), VEIL VPN, ETHastic (offline radio mesh payments), Solana ATM, Unruggable (hardware). Arc explicitly praised rails that "work when everything else fails." Judges remember a demo they can picture.
3. **Prediction and speculation consumer apps keep winning Consumer tracks.** Pregame (Radar), Trepa and Melee Markets (Breakout), Capitola and Fora (Cypherpunk), EigenBets, Goddid.Money, Predict It!, PolyPOP. MBC 2025 also had a Polymarket bounty. The winning versions add a social or novel mechanic (group chat, X tags, sentiment, aggregation), not just another order book.
4. **Payments to real-world money with a named market or geography win Payments tracks.** FXSwap, Nomad (Nigeria), Ripe (SE Asia), CargoBill (supply chains), LocalPay, Credible (remittances to India), Sp3nd (Amazon). The pitch names who pays whom and why stablecoins beat the status quo.
5. **Mechanism novelty plus an end-to-end flow beats a feature.** Yumi does BNPL from underwriting through origination. Urani protects against MEV with intents. Ore introduced a new PoW design. InfoFi lets buyers pay only if a ZK-verified accuracy threshold is met. Judges reward a new primitive *shown working*, not a wrapper.
6. **Working demo and production seriousness.** Colosseum says to prioritize "an amazing working demo on devnet" with an "aha" moment. KeeperHub's top pick had 125 tests, a live deployment and real payment flows. ZW.ARM ran with real money on mainnet. **What loses:** "surface integrations... no real agent loop," "the path of least resistance to meet the integration requirement," and "a compelling demo that does not survive contact with a real codebase."
7. **A startup framing helps at Colosseum-style judging.** The criteria favor founders who "intend to build full-time" with a viable business model, evidence of real users or traction, founder-market fit and an ambitious long-term vision. The pitch is a startup pitch, not a feature tour.
8. **Student panels and community votes reward emotional, human, instantly-graspable hooks.** FaceBuddy ("everyone has a face") won a celebrity judge pick, 1st in community vote and 6 bounties. zkHotdog (prove an object's size) was 2nd in community vote. Bot or Not (Turing test meets Among Us) was a finalist. **Bounty stacking** pays: FaceBuddy and Cyspace each collected 3-6 sponsor prizes with one project.
9. **Clear, non-buzzword chain integration.** Colosseum's workshop says losers have "unclear Solana integration," vague or buzzword descriptions, flashy visuals with little substance, videos over time, or no team background. Judges are "particularly interested in the reasoning behind" technical choices, such as why the logic sits onchain and why Solana.

## (c) Published judging guidance and comments found

- **Colosseum, "How to Win a Colosseum Hackathon"** (https://blog.colosseum.com/how-to-win-a-colosseum-hackathon/): judged only on work done during the hackathon. Prizes go to teams intending to go full-time with viable business models. Build a working devnet demo with an "aha" moment. Founder-market fit. New markets, or existing markets brought onchain. Winning teams average more than 3 people and mix technical and non-technical members. Pitch video under 3 minutes covering team, product, why, market, traction and walkthrough; it is "the most important component in rising above the crowd."
- **Colosseum, "Perfecting Your Hackathon Submission"** (https://blog.colosseum.com/perfecting-your-hackathon-submission/): evidence of a real problem and real users (early traction, user conversations, feedback from X or Telegram). Explain the reasoning behind technical decisions. "A clear and well-structured narrative is more valuable than professional video editing." Treat it as a startup pitch. Pitfalls: going over time, buzzwords, unclear Solana integration, missing repo or doc access, unpolished videos.
- **Colosseum winner posts** (Renaissance, Radar, Breakout, Cypherpunk) publish **no per-project judge rationale**. The Colosseum team syncs with judges and chooses after individual evaluations.
- **Arc / Circle (ETHGlobal Cannes)**: criteria were "alignment with bounty requirements, quality of solution, depth of technical implementation, and creativity." Lessons: agents as the primary users of financial infrastructure, nanopayments as core rather than bolted on, resilience and off-grid use, programmable creator payouts, impact (emergency finance, privacy, artist pay), and mainnet plans. Very relevant because **Circle/Arc sponsors UBC 2026**. (Note: the post's winner count is internally inconsistent.)
- **KeeperHub (ETHGlobal Open Agents)**: rewards production seriousness, reusable SDK design, and an independent critic agent that checks each decision before execution ("failure-mode thinking"). Penalizes passive, webhook-only, check-the-box integrations.
- **MBC 2025 (UBC predecessor)**: no published rubric. The Devpost blurb emphasizes technical depth, originality, strong UX and real-world impact. The top 10 pitch live. UBC 2026's own criteria (Technical Execution, Product & UX, Innovation, Impact & Potential, Demo) map closely onto these.
- **Devfolio generic rubric** (used by many ETH events): Technicality, Originality, Practicality, Usability, Wow factor. https://guide.devfolio.co/docs/guide/judging
